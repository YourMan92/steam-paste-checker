# Per-language checks: cultural, genre, typography

Run the full section for each language. "Flag" means note it for the buyer. Don't change their game's content.

## All languages
- [ ] Genre vocabulary chosen deliberately (table below). No loot-shooter or survival terms for non-combat games.
- [ ] The buyer's avoid list is mapped to local equivalents (table below) and absent.
- [ ] USD prices removed from localized copy (Steam shows regional prices). Flag it.
- [ ] Dates: keep only in About, never in the short description. Use "scheduled" wording if the date may slip.
- [ ] Comparable game titles: use the official localized title (check `store.steampowered.com/app/<id>/?l=<code>`), otherwise keep English and flag "not verified."
- [ ] If the game itself isn't localized into the language, suggest an "English only" line (don't add it silently).
- [ ] Sensitive themes for that market (below). Flag pairings, don't censor.
- [ ] Idioms, wordplay and puns: adapt and mark Δ. Never leave an untranslatable pun literal.
- [ ] Tagline and capsule text: short. Under about 12 CJK characters is ideal for a capsule.

## Simplified Chinese (zh-Hans)
- Typography: full-width punctuation `，。！？：；“”（）`. No spaces between Chinese characters. One space around Latin words or numbers is acceptable but be consistent (default: none around numbers, a space around English titles).
- Game titles in Chinese text: 《…》 if a Chinese title is used. English titles in `[i]` with spaces around them.
- Register: neutral written Chinese (书面语). Avoid internet slang on the store page (slang is fine in social copy).
- Sensitive for the PRC audience (flag only): maps and borders (Taiwan, South China Sea), skeletons or blood imagery in marketing (historically sensitive for China publishing; Steam global isn't affected), political figures, gambling wording (赌博). Store translation **isn't** China publishing approval (版号). Never imply the game is approved in China.
- Steam CN players value: clear genre, playtime, "has Chinese" (有中文). If the game has **no** in-game Chinese, say so to avoid refunds and bad reviews ("不支持中文" reviews are common).

## Japanese (ja)
- Typography: full-width punctuation `、。！？「」『』`. Japanese uses 「」 for quotes, and 『』 for titles if a Japanese title is used. The wave dash `～` for ranges (6～8時間).
- Register: pick one. **だ／である** (literary, moody) or **です／ます** (friendly, neutral). Never mix (the checker warns). Calls to action: 〜しよう／〜してください, matching the register.
- Subjects are usually dropped, which naturally solves EN "I/you" inconsistencies.
- Katakana loanwords: prefer established ones (サルベージ, ローグライク). Don't over-katakana normal words.
- Sensitive (flag): tsunami or earthquake imagery (2011), so avoid 津波 unless the game is about it. Wartime and imperial history. Overt religious satire.
- JP players read reviews closely and dislike overclaiming. Keep superlatives out.

## Korean (ko)
- Typography: Korean uses spaces (띄어쓰기). Check spacing in compound nouns. Quotes: '…' or "…". Ranges: 6~8시간.
- Register: **합니다체** for descriptions, **~세요** for calls to action. The plain **다** form only for slogans or taglines.
- Loanwords follow the National Institute of Korean Language romanization habits used by Steam KR (로그라이크, 메트로배니아).
- Sensitive (flag): ferry or ship sinking imagery (2014 Sewol), Japan–Korea historical themes, the Rising Sun motif in art, North Korea references.
- KR players use 파밍 (looting), 힐링 (cozy) and 감성 (mood) heavily. Map them to the avoid list deliberately.
- Steam language code is **koreana**.
